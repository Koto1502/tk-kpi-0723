Revenue: what is happening and why

Tiny Knight Fall · 21 Oct 2026 · weeks are Monday–Sunday · GA4 through 18 Oct, Play through 18 Oct, AppsFlyer through 20 Oct · excludes China

Summary

  • Revenue per player is flat; players are disappearing. IAP per DAU per day has had no trend since late August; DAU fell 1,160 → 455.
  • The 12–18 Oct drop is real (Play net IAP $889 → $609) and partly the end of the v36 launch spike. Our report and the publisher’s disagreed only because of different week boundaries; both now use Monday–Sunday.
  • v34 (25 Sep) raised difficulty; v36 (10 Oct) rebalanced it. Separately, D1 fell from 26% to 20% from 12 Oct among non-Tier-1 players on v36 — not explained by difficulty or traffic mix.
  • Payers keep playing but stop buying; whales burn through the catalogue in ~5 days. The shop lacks offers for week 2 onward.
  • D7 ROAS is 35% because a paid install earns ~$0.60 by day 7 against a $1.75 average CPI; only GA Global ($1.12) comes close.
  • Removing the no-ads sale pack cost ~59% of no-ads buyers with no trade-up to $29.99. Restore the $10.99 pack.

Which number to quote

  • IAP revenue → Play net (ex-tax, minus refunds). It is Play’s accounting record, one row per order. The publisher report’s “IAP after tax” is within ±15% of it every week (ratio 0.83–1.09), so the two agree on direction. GA4 is telemetry: it includes VAT and never sees a refund, so it runs 5–15% above Play.
  • Ad revenue → the MAX dashboard figure (as printed in the publisher report). GA4 records only 20–45% of it (2.3–5.0× gap, narrowing as newer builds log more impressions); AppsFlyer covers only attributed installs. Use GA4 ad revenue for per-player comparisons only.
  • Spend → AppsFlyer. It matches the publisher report to the dollar every week.
  • Behaviour (DAU, retention, payers, stages) → GA4. The GA4 export stopped on 19 Oct 13:18 UTC; 18 Oct is the last complete day.

Why two reports disagreed about 12–18 Oct

Our weekly report ran Friday–Thursday and showed IAP +68% (9–15 Oct). The publisher report runs Monday–Sunday and showed −27% (12–18 Oct). Both were correct: the v36 launch spike on 9–11 Oct ($240, $172, $280 GA4 IAP per day) sat inside our current week and inside their previous week. From now on every report uses Monday–Sunday weeks.

What the trend actually is

IAP per DAU per day has held at roughly $0.13–0.31 (no trend) since late August. Revenue is falling because DAU is falling: 1,160 → 455 (−61%) as UA spend was cut and retention stayed low. The money problem is a players problem.

Why D1 fell from 12 Oct (26.3% → 20.0%)

It is not the difficulty and not the traffic mix. Players reach stage 10 as often as before (40% → 40%), and weighting 12–17 Oct installs back to the 1–9 Oct campaign mix still gives 20.3%. What changed is the return on day 1 among players who played normally on day 0: D1 for players who reached stage 10 fell 37.5% → 31.6%.

3. The questions, and why

What will increase revenue, in order

  1. Find and fix the v36 day-1 drop (D1 26% → 20% for non-Tier-1 players). Fewer players is the whole revenue decline; this is the newest loss.
  2. Sell to payers who are still playing. 55% of payers play 3+ days the next week but only 38% of them buy again. Add rotating daily offers, a week-2 membership/pass upsell, and progress-keyed offers.
  3. Give whales a growing sink. They exhaust the catalogue in about 5 days and leave. High-tier recurring offers and weekly banners.
  4. Restore the $10.99 permanent no-ads pack (~$69/week at current installs) and fix the 72-hour auto-refund bug.
  5. Buy installs where they can pay back: GA ROAS Global (49% D7 on GA4 revenue at $1.12 CPI) over Tier 1 ($5–11 CPI, 37%). Fix the Facebook revenue postback before judging FB again.
  6. Review the v34 difficulty walls (stages 21–31, 54–56, 79–100): new players reach stage 60 less often than on v29.

“Revenue after the first purchase is low” — partly. Payers stay; they stop buying.

  • Of 736 payers, 82% come back in the 7 days after their first purchase and 55% play on 3 or more of those days. Payers retain 10× better than non-payers (D7 41% vs 4.3%).
  • But only 38% of the payers who keep playing buy again that week — one purchase per 9 active days.
  • Payers who eventually leave keep playing a median 7 days after their last purchase; 51% play a week or more without buying.
  • What sells after day 1 is recurring value: daily/weekly/monthly offers, membership and passes are 47% of later revenue vs 22% on the first day. Top later sellers: membership $812, daily.challenge.key.1 $693, pass.tiny.guide $474, package.combo.mythic $430, selective.hero $284.

Why: the first-day shop converts (starter, no-ads and power packs), but an engaged payer on days 2–7 is shown little that is new or time-limited. The fix is offer cadence for payers who are still playing — a rotating daily deal, a second-week membership or pass upsell, offers keyed to progress — not more first-purchase offers.

Why D7 ROAS is not 100%

Across all paid cohorts AppsFlyer puts D7 ROAS at 35%: a paid install costs $1.75 on average and returns about $0.61 by day 7. D7 revenue per install = payer rate × ARPPU + ads, and for 100% it has to equal CPI. Anything above ~$1 CPI loses money at current monetisation. Two levers: buy where CPI is ≤ $1.20 (Global), and raise payer rate and day 2–7 spending (see above). Tier 1 at $5–11 CPI cannot work today.

Whale patterns (lifetime ≥ $100)

  • 33 players = 33% of IAP revenue; 12 are in the US. 25 of 33 have left.
  • They spend early and briefly: day 0 34%, 1-2 21%, 3-6 21%, 7-13 10%, 14-29 8%, 30+ 7% of their spend by day since install. Median buying span: 5 days.
  • They race through content: median stage by day 3 is 172 for whales vs 62 for other payers and 13 for non-payers; at the end they are at stage ~376.
  • They stop buying before they stop playing: 40% of churned whales played a week or more after their last purchase.
  • Only 3 of the 33 installed on v34 or later. Newer players have had less time to reach $100, but most whales crossed it within a week, so new whales have largely stopped arriving.

Why: whales buy power to progress fast, run out of meaningful things to buy within about a week, and leave a few days later. They need a sink that grows with them — high-tier recurring offers (membership tiers, a premium pass with late rewards), gacha/hero banners that refresh weekly, and offers keyed to stage 150+ — rather than the starter-shop catalogue they have already bought.

Do events help?

Event and pass packs earned $1,392 (7.3% of IAP), $857 of it the Tiny Guide pass. To see whether an event changes behaviour, each participant is compared with non-participants who spent and played the same amount before the event (matched on spend bracket, active days before, active days during):

  • Lucky Machine did not raise spending during the event (participants’ spend fell as much as their matches’), but participants stayed more active afterwards (8.1 vs 5.6 days in the next 14) and spent more after it ended — a retention effect, on 32 matched players.
  • Flameborn pass: matched participants spent $2.12 more per player during the window — a modest lift, on 28 players, in the same week as the v36 pack launch.
  • Game-wide IAP per DAU did not move during Lucky Machine ($0.218 vs $0.211 the two weeks before).

Why: events reach only the engaged 10–20% (213 spinners, 9.5% of eligible), and their packs are priced and placed as extras. They work as retention for players who are already staying; they do not create new spenders. The upcoming Dice and Fishing events should be judged on participant retention and pack conversion among stage ≥ 101 players, and need their pack hookup and tracking in place at launch.

Did removing the no-ads sale pack (v32, 21 Sep) work? No.

  • No-ads buyers fell by 59%. The $10.99 permanent pack sold 92 times in the first period; the $29.99 permanent pack went from 10 to 5 sales — nobody traded up. Some moved to the $10.99 30-day pack (8 → 18), which refunds at 32%.
  • At 833 installs a week that is about $69/week of no-ads revenue. Other IAP rose by $0.063 per install over the same period, but that came with the v34/v36 packs, not from former no-ads buyers.

Why: the $10.99 permanent pack is a first-48-hours impulse buy for price-sensitive new players. Removing it left a $29.99 price those players will not pay, so they bought nothing or a worse-value 30-day pack. Recommendation unchanged since 10 Oct: restore the $10.99 permanent pack as the new-player offer, and fix the purchase-acknowledgement bug that auto-refunds one-time purchases at 72 hours.

Method and sources

  • Population: all players except China (third-party v29 APK traffic from 19 Sep, no revenue). GA4 data through 18 Oct; Play orders through 18 Oct; AppsFlyer through 20 Oct.
  • Play amounts are converted to USD with a per-currency rate taken from 315 orders matched to GA4 purchases (same SKU, within 5 minutes); 3% of orders in 7 rare currencies have no rate and are left out.
  • Queries and scripts: dashboard/rev1021_sql/user_days.sql (one row per player-day), notebooks/rev1021/money_weeks.py, notebooks/rev1021/deep_why.py, notebooks/rev1021/analysis.py, builder dashboard/build_truth_report.py. Stage data: parquet/stage_timing.parquet (stages report extract).
  • Revenue figures are gross unless marked Play net. Small groups (whales n=33, matched event players n=28–32) are directional.
