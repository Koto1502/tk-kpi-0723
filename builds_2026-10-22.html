What each build changed — and what it did

The short version

  1. v25 is the one clear regression — and it was the build, not the traffic. D1 −2.6pt, D7 −2.3pt and revenue per install about −50% against v24, and all three survive mix adjustment and dropping the top payer. The 17/07 Tier 1 campaign (47% of all payers) did stop two days before v25 shipped, but correcting for traffic mix barely changes the drop — the payment bug is the better explanation. v26.2 fixed it: D7 8.2%, the best of the window.
  2. v34's harder stages cost about 2.5pt of D1 (24.6% vs 27.1% on v29, mix-adjusted). Its higher revenue per install is only partly real: +60% vs v29 with the top payer dropped, about +21% once traffic mix is equalised — the 29/09 Tier 1 ROAS campaign started four days into v34.
  3. v36 (Daily Quest + Battle Pass) did not deliver — and it made the early game harder. Its “campaign rebalance” moved the walls earlier: a new wall at stage 34 (losses 10% → 40%) plus 50–53 and 57–60, while removing the old 54–56 wall. D1 −3.2pt (21.4% mix-adjusted, the lowest of the window, on 1,243 installs); revenue per player-day only +3%. v37 made stage 34 harder still (45% losses, clear time doubled). v36's D7 and first-week revenue cannot be judged yet: the export gap leaves only 32 v36 installs with seven observed days.
  4. v37 is v36 pushed to everyone — same features, but stage 34 got harder again. Its D1 is +2.9pt raw but only +0.9pt mix-adjusted (the rest is the install wave from 10-21), and fewer of its players get past stage 34 (12% → 8% within 3 days, early). Treat ARPU swings under ±40% and D1 swings under ±1pt as noise: v19 and v33 changed nothing visible and moved that much.

Since v20: did the new features raise LTV? no — and the data says why

Between v20 (3 Aug) and v37 the game added Sugar World, finished Astrology, then Heroes, Alchemy, Toys, Treasure, pets, the Loot/Village loop, the Lucky Machine event, Battle Pass (three versions), Daily Quest, a campaign rebalance and 30+ new IAP packs. First-week revenue per install did not go up once: v20's $0.70 is still the best D7 LTV of the window, and later builds sit between $0.16 and $0.68 (top payer dropped). v34's $0.68 is mostly the 29/09 Tier 1 campaign — about +21% over v29 once traffic mix is equalised.

Why not

  1. Fewer players pay; payers do not spend less. The D7 payer rate fell from 3.0–3.3% (v20–21) to 1.3–2.5% afterwards, while the median first-week spend per payer stayed at $10–14. The lost LTV is lost payers.
  2. Part of that is traffic. The 17/07 Tier 1 ROAS campaign (47% of all payers) fed v17–v24 and stopped on 09-03. Equalising the traffic mix closes some of the gap, but builds after v24 are still ~25–30% below v20–v24.
  3. The new content sits where few players arrive. Within 5 days only 15–19% of installs reach stage 31 and 3–7% reach stage 100 — and that share has not grown. Alchemy, Loot Storage and the campaign packs unlock deeper still (stage 115 / 200 per the roadmap), so 9 in 10 installs never see most of what shipped. Meanwhile D1 sits at 21–28%: the game loses three quarters of its players before the new systems can matter.
  4. Many packs barely sell, or never reached players. Talisman, The Nun, hero-soul and Hope packs show no purchases; the hero growth pack is clicked by 0.4% and the premium pass bought by 0.4%. The Battle Pass sat in the build for three weeks before anyone could use it (dev v30 → device v36).
  5. Ads grew, IAP shrank. Ad revenue per install rose 2.7× (8% → 24% of first-week revenue), helped by removing the cheap No-Ads offers — but it does not make up the IAP lost to falling conversion.
  6. Difficulty kept moving the wrong way. v25's payment bug (D7 LTV $0.24) aside, v34 raised the walls (D1 −2.5pt), and v36's “rebalance” moved them earlier — a new wall at stage 34, where far more players arrive — which v37 made harder again. Each step trims the share of installs who ever reach the content built for them.

What this points to: work on the D0–D1 funnel and first-purchase conversion in the first ~30 stages, where every install actually is — not more late-game systems and packs.

How to read this — definitions and caveats
  • Builds are the 17 releases that reached ≥30% of daily players (v37 is still rolling out). Rollout = first day seen → 20% → 50% of daily players. Stragglers and test builds (27.2, 30, 31, 35, 350) are left out of the columns. Device v35 is one of those test builds (12 players, 10-04 → 10-09): the content the dev calls “v35” reached players as device v36.
  • Install cohort = players whose first session was on that build. Retention and ARPU are cohort metrics, so a build is judged on the new players it received — not on veterans who updated into it (they are more engaged and would flatter newer builds).
  • ARPU D7 = revenue in the first 8 days (install day + 7) ÷ installs old enough to have had them. D1 / D7 = share of installs active exactly 1 / 7 days after install (the cards and table also show every day D3–D7, for retention and for LTV = cumulative revenue per install by that day). ARPDAU = revenue ÷ player-days, by the build actually running that day — the one metric that includes veterans.
  • Mix-adjusted re-weights each build's cohort to the same traffic mix — geo tier (US / other Tier 1 / rest of world) × traffic type (ROAS Tier 1 campaigns / other paid / organic / unattributed), using the window average of the selected segment and only groups with ≥20 installs. When raw and adjusted disagree, the movement came from who was installing, not from the build. ARPDAU is never mix-adjusted or whale-trimmed: it covers every player on the build, veterans included.
  • Drop top payer removes the single biggest IAP spender from each build's cohort. Revenue at this scale is whale-dominated; a change that disappears with one player removed is not a build effect.
  • Faded values have too few installs to trust (<200 for ARPU, or a retention change that is not statistically distinguishable from zero at 95%). Blank = not enough mature installs yet.
  • Campaign comes from AppsFlyer: about 45% of installs match a paid campaign; the rest are Organic (matched to AppsFlyer's organic list) or Unattributed. Filtering to one campaign samples it — it never covers it fully.
  • Overlap warnings fire when a campaign starts or stops, or a store / live-event change happens, between 3 days before and 10 days after a build's first day.
  • Android only (the iOS app is not in the GA4 export). China excluded. Revenue is gross (no refund events), and IAP tracking began 2026-07-20. The window ends 10-25. The export is missing or nearly empty on 09-28 and 10-18 → 10-20 (grey bands): those days are unobserved — installs on them are dropped, no retention day lands on them, and players who installed before 10-18 are followed only to 10-17. That is why v36 has fewer mature installs, and no mix-adjusted D7/ARPU (too few per traffic group).
