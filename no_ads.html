The no-ads sale packs

Did v28 remove them, and what would removing them cost?
tiny-knightfall-idle-rpg-game · GA4 analytics_538412813 + live Play storefront · generated 2026-09-18

They were not removed. Two new builds did ship — v28 on 2026-09-16 and v29 on 2026-09-17 — but players on both are still opening and completing purchases of skip.ad.permanent.sale, most recently 2026-09-18. All three .sale SKUs are still ACTIVE / AVAILABLE on the live Play storefront, and the 2026-09-15 export from game source still specifies them.

And they are worth far more than they look. Ad-removal SKUs are 36.7% of all tracked IAP revenue; the sale variants alone are 24.6%. Over the last 28 days permanent.sale by itself was 22% of total revenue. Removing it is a bet on roughly a fifth of the game's income.

Versions are opaque strings in this export — v26 stamps itself 26.2, so any integer cast silently drops the newest release. Three builds are newer than v26.2:

v28 is the release, live 2026-09-16, with v29 a day later. 27.2 reached seven devices on one day and looks internal. Combined adoption is still only ~500 devices against v24's 3,280 — this player base updates slowly, which caps what three days of data can say.

2 · The packs are still being sold on those builds

Four independent signals can answer this, and they are not equally strong. purchase_click and iap_purchase_success are client custom events — good evidence the shop still shows the card, but in principle a stale logging call could outlive the UI. in_app_purchase is the Firebase SDK auto-log from Play Billing — it fires on the billing result regardless of what the client shop is displaying, so on its own it cannot prove the offer exists. The decisive one is earn: earn.position carries the SKU id for shop grants, so it is the client actually paying out the reward.

The live Play catalogue was re-pulled for this report and diffed against the copy committed on 2026-09-16: 82 products, nothing added, removed, repriced or deactivated. Worth checking whether the change actually made the build that was submitted — v29 is the newest build in the data and it is still selling them.

Two ways the data could have lied here, both ruled out. Mis-stamped versions (a purchase started before the update and logged after it): no — all nine users involved appear on exactly one build, with first_open on that same build, so none of them straddles an update. QA devices: no — they are nine separate installs in nine countries (Switzerland, Spain, Poland, United States ×2, Germany, Indonesia, South Korea, Hong Kong).

One free win: skip.ad.annual.sale has never been tapped or bought once in the entire export, despite being active on the storefront. If the goal is pruning dead sale SKUs, that is the genuinely dead one — and it costs nothing to remove.

4 · Why "they'll just buy the $29.99 one" is unlikely

This price point already moved once. At v21 (2026-08-08) the offer mix flipped from mostly-$29.99 to mostly-$10.99: revenue for the $29.99 option fell ~70% per capita, but combined buyers per 1,000 users rose, 9.3 → 10.5. That is price expanding the market, not cannibalising it — the $10.99 buyers were largely people who were never going to pay $29.99. No user has ever bought both SKUs.

So the question is what share of sale buyers would re-convert at 2.7× the price. Applied to the last 28 days ($1,223 across ~111 purchases):

More than ~37% of sale buyers must convert at the higher price just to break even, and the v21 episode points the other way. This is arithmetic on a bracket, not a forecast — but it frames the bet honestly, and the downside is a fifth of total revenue.

5 · A trap: do not read v28's ARPDAU as the effect

Over the same three days v28 shows roughly 4.5× v26.2's revenue per 1,000 DAU and ten times the ad-removal tap rate. None of that is caused by the release. Three days after a rollout, the people on the new build are the ones whose auto-update fired first — which correlates with opening the game daily, i.e. the engaged core, who are also the payers. Everyone still on v26.2 is the laggard tail. The comparison measures who updates fast, not what changed in the build.

A clean read needs install-date cohorts matched across builds, or simply waiting until v28/v29 reach majority adoption — about a week here, judging by v25 and v26.2.

6 · What to do

  • Confirm the change shipped at all. Three surfaces say it did not. Check the submitted build before assuming the data is wrong.
  • Remove annual.sale now — zero taps, zero revenue, zero risk.
  • Do not remove permanent.sale in one shot. It is ~22% of revenue. Stage it: drop 30days.sale first, or run it as a price test rather than a deletion.
  • Wait a week before scoring any of it. Three days and ~500 devices cannot separate the change from update-speed selection.
Revenue is gross — this export has no refund or cancellation event, so reconcile against Play Console before booking any figure.
