Should we revert the no-ads sale-pack removal?

v32 (live 2026-09-21) removed skip.ad.permanent.sale ($10.99), skip.ad.30days.sale ($4.99) and skip.ad.annual.sale ($4.99). This is the decision document: install-cohort × matched days-since-install, ex-China, to 2026-10-10 — 19 post-removal days and 2,287 v32+ installs, up from 255 installs / 3 payers on 2026-09-25. Supersedes the 2026-09-25 early read that previously held this page name.

Verdict: keep it removed — do not revert now, and fix the acknowledgement bug first

The removal is revenue-neutral within the precision this data supports, and it did not fix the refund defect it is usually credited with. Reverting would re-add a SKU whose orders refund at 9.4% before that defect is fixed, in exchange for a revenue difference smaller than the measurement error. That makes this a conditional keep: ship the fix, then re-test the price point.

  • Net revenue effect: indistinguishable from zero. D0–D2 +0.0277 $/install (90% CI -0.1382 … +0.1993), campaign-mix reweighted +0.0018; D0–D7 -0.0863 (CI -0.2949 … +0.1322). At 843 installs/week that is −$73 to $23/week — against the $241/week of .sale revenue that vanished (ex-China, offer-present window).
  • The break-even test fails outright. The $29.99 twin needs ≥36.6% of .sale buyers to re-convert. Observed: full-price buyers went 1.9 → 1.5 per 1,000 installs, i.e. -3.6% (CI -18.9% … +12.4%) — the interval excludes break-even in every window. Play's accounting record agrees: the $29.99 SKU sold 14 orders before the removal and 8 after.
  • What replaced it is a worse product at the same price. The lost volume went to the surviving skip.ad.30days pack: the same $10.99 now buys 30 days of no ads instead of permanent — Play orders 10 → 19, and it refunds at 31.6% (Fisher p = 0.026 vs the removed SKU's pre-removal rate).
  • Reverting buys no refund relief, and neither does keeping it removed. The game's order refund rate rose from 3.7% to 5.4% while the non-ad-removal catalogue was flat (2.3% → 2.9%). The defect is real and unfixed: 10 Google-initiated refunds, every one exactly 72.17 h after the charge — Play auto-refunding purchases the client never acknowledged — and 6 of the 10 are on the surviving full-price SKUs.
  • Players are hunting for the offer and hitting a wall: no-ads card taps 25.6 → 73.4 per 1,000 installs (2.9×), and full-price card taps 9.7 → 28.7.

The condition: fix the unacknowledged-purchase path, then re-run this page. If refunds on the surviving ad-removal SKUs clear and the D0–D7 revenue gap is still there, restore one $10.99 permanent pack — not the three-SKU discount bundle. The strongest counter-argument, and what would falsify this verdict, is in section 7.

-- Did the removal ship, and on which build? Every no-ads signal by app version,
-- 2026-09-14 .. 2026-10-12 (v32 went live 2026-09-21).
-- `earn` is the decisive column: `position` holds the SKU for shop grants, so it is the
-- client paying out the reward and cannot fire from a shop card that no longer exists.
WITH e AS (
  SELECT app_info.version v, event_name, user_pseudo_id u, PARSE_DATE('%Y%m%d', event_date) d,
    event_value_in_usd usd,
    COALESCE((SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='product_id'),
             (SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='position')) sku
  FROM `tiny-knightfall-idle-rpg-game.analytics_538412813.events_intraday_*`
  WHERE _TABLE_SUFFIX BETWEEN '20260914' AND '20261012'
)
SELECT v AS version, MIN(d) first_day, MAX(d) last_day,
  COUNT(DISTINCT u) users,
  COUNTIF(event_name='first_open') installs,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='purchase_click')  sale_taps,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='in_app_purchase') sale_buys,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='earn')            sale_grants,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='iap_purchase_success') sale_success,
  ROUND(SUM(IF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='in_app_purchase', usd, 0)), 2) sale_usd,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='purchase_click')   full_taps,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='in_app_purchase')  full_buys,
  ROUND(SUM(IF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='in_app_purchase', usd, 0)), 2) full_usd,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad.annual.sale%' AND event_name IN ('purchase_click','in_app_purchase','earn')) annual_sale_signals,
  MAX(IF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='in_app_purchase', d, NULL)) last_sale_buy
FROM e
GROUP BY v
HAVING users >= 5
ORDER BY first_day

-- Ad-removal SKU revenue by ISO week (Mon-start) and SKU, 2026-08-01 .. 2026-10-12.
-- This is the revenue side of the removal: which SKUs carried it, when they stopped,

)
SELECT DATE_TRUNC(d, WEEK(MONDAY)) wk,
  REPLACE(sku, 'com.idle.tiny.', '') sku,
  IF(country = 'China', 'China', 'rest') geo_group,
  COUNTIF(event_name = 'purchase_click') taps,

2 · Design — install cohorts at matched days since install

Claim. Because the offer only ever sold to players in their first few days, the fair comparison is install cohorts scored over the same days-since-install window, with China excluded and only completed windows counted. Active-user or version cuts are not usable here.

ArmInstall datesInstalls (ex-China)Note
Offer present2026-09-04 … 2026-09-204,107every build live then (v21–v29) sold the packs
Offer absent2026-09-21 … 2026-10-071,988installs whose `first_open` build is v32+
Offer absent, D0–D72026-09-21 … 2026-10-021,409smaller: only installs whose day 7 had happened by 10-09
Offer absent, gap-free D0–D72026-09-21 … 10-021,307excludes anyone whose window contains the 09-28 outage
Offer absent, all builds2026-09-21 … 2026-10-072,318includes 255 old-build (v29) installs that still show the offer

Windows are D0, D0–D2 and D0–D7; each arm is restricted to installs whose whole window finished by 2026-10-09 (10-10 is an intraday, partial shard), so the D0–D7 arm is smaller than the D0–D2 arm by construction — which is why the two must never be compared to each other, only to their own offer-present baseline. Two traps are handled explicitly:

  • China is excluded from both arms. The 09-19 mirror/bot spike is ~300 installs/day on v29 with $0 IAP and $0 ads; left in, it would make the post arm look better and v29 look terrible. It is still 9.7% of post-removal installs (255), so the all-builds row above exists to show it makes no difference.
  • The 2026-09-28 09:00 → 09-29 03:00 UTC event gap (~18h, no events at all) sits inside the D0–D7 window of installs from 09-21 to 09-27, i.e. only the post arm. The gap-free row removes exactly those installs; the gap can only understate post revenue, which is the conservative direction for this verdict.
The per-user-day extract (5.0 GiB scan, cached):
-- Per user-day facts, 2026-08-01 .. 2026-10-12 (one row per device per active day).
-- Source: GA4 raw export, events_intraday_* shards only. Extended from
-- notebooks/sql/15_user_day_facts.sql with the no-ads SKU classes split out, so the
-- substitution question (did .sale buyers move to the full-price twin?) can be answered
-- on the same per-install-day grid without a second scan.
WITH ev AS (
  SELECT user_pseudo_id u, PARSE_DATE('%Y%m%d', event_date) d, event_name e,
    event_timestamp ts, geo.country country, app_info.version v, event_value_in_usd usd,
    -- `earn` carries the SKU in `position`, purchases in `product_id`; take both, in this order
    COALESCE((SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='product_id'),
             (SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='position')) sku,
    -- Impression-level ad revenue reported by AppLovin MAX.
    (SELECT COALESCE(value.double_value, value.float_value, CAST(value.int_value AS FLOAT64))
       FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='value') val,
    (SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(user_properties) WHERE key='appsflyer_id') af
  FROM `tiny-knightfall-idle-rpg-game.analytics_538412813.events_intraday_*`
  WHERE _TABLE_SUFFIX BETWEEN '20260801' AND '20261012'
)
SELECT u, d,
  ANY_VALUE(country) country,
  ARRAY_AGG(v IGNORE NULLS ORDER BY ts DESC LIMIT 1)[SAFE_OFFSET(0)] v,
  MAX(af) af,
  COUNTIF(e='first_open') first_opens,
  -- all IAP
  SUM(IF(e='in_app_purchase', usd, 0)) iap_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='in_app_purchase') iap_buys,
  -- ad-removal SKUs, all of them
  SUM(IF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%', usd, 0)) noads_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%') noads_buys,
  -- the discounted .sale variants removed in v32
  SUM(IF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale', usd, 0)) sale_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale') sale_buys,
  -- the full-price twin of the pack that mattered: skip.ad.permanent at $29.99
  SUM(IF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad.permanent%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale', usd, 0)) perm_full_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad.permanent%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale') perm_full_buys,
  -- the single pack that carried the volume: skip.ad.permanent.sale at $10.99
  SUM(IF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad.permanent.sale%', usd, 0)) perm_sale_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad.permanent.sale%') perm_sale_buys,
  -- card taps: purchase_click, the player opening the offer
  COUNTIF(e='purchase_click' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%') noads_taps,
  COUNTIF(e='purchase_click' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad.permanent%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale') perm_full_taps,
  COUNTIF(e='purchase_click' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale') sale_taps,
  COUNTIF(e='purchase_click') shop_taps,
  -- ad revenue (impression-level) and impressions
  SUM(IF(e='ad_impression_MAX', COALESCE(val, 0), 0)) ad_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='ad_impression_MAX') ad_imps
FROM ev
GROUP BY u, d

4 · The crux — did .sale buyers trade up to the $29.99 twin?

Claim: no, and not close. Break-even on removing a $10.99 pack in favour of its identical-reward $29.99 twin needs ≥36.6% of the .sale buyers to re-convert at 2.7× the price. The measured trade-up is -3.6% (90% CI -18.9% … +12.4%), and the interval excludes 36.6% in every window. In Play's accounting record the $29.99 twin's orders fell across the removal (14 → 8). The volume moved to the surviving 30-day pack at the same $10.99.

5 · Net per install, per week, against the revenue that vanished

Claim. The .sale line was worth $241/week (ex-China, offer-present window). The net effect on the game is between −$73/week (D0–D7) and $23/week (D0–D2), with a 90% interval of −$116 to $168/week at the current 843 installs/week — i.e. indistinguishable from zero either way, and far smaller than the gross line that disappeared because most of it was replaced.

Where the replacement came from (D0–D2, $/install): the lost $0.15 of .sale revenue was more than offset in the raw figures by the surviving no-ads SKUs $0.05 ($0.01 … $0.08, significant — it is skip.ad.30days picking up), other IAP $0.06 (−$0.04 … $0.18, not significant) and ad revenue $0.07 ($0.04 … $0.10, significant).

But a large part of the ad gain is not attributable to the removal. Splitting it: impressions per 1,000 installs 1,043.3 → 1,368.7 (+31%), but revenue per 1,000 impressions $60.32 → $96.39 (+60%). Removing a shop card cannot raise revenue per impression — that is fill/eCPM, and this build is known to have much better rewarded fill than v29 (70–76% vs 50%, 2026-10-02 weekly report). Per install build it is plain: the offer-present arm's ad revenue is $0.05 on v24 and $0.04 on v25, while the offer-absent arm is $0.15 on v34 (70% of it). The arms' build mixes differ, so the ad line is confounded — treat the D0–D2 "neutral" reading as optimistic and the "ad line set to zero" bound above as the honest pessimistic one.

6 · Refunds — the finding that is entangled with this decision

Claim. Two distinct delivery defects are visible in Play's own records, and the removal fixed neither of them. It removed one SKU that refunded at 9.4% and replaced its volume with a SKU that refunds at 31.6% (Fisher p = 0.026). The game's refund rate went up, 3.7% → 5.4%, and the increase is entirely inside the ad-removal family (non-ad-removal: 2.3% → 2.9%).

Defect 1 — the client never acknowledges one-time-product purchases. 10 refunds in the ledger were initiated by Google, and every single one lands 72.17 h (3 days + 10 minutes) after the charge — a machine, not a player. That is the signature of Play's automatic refund for a purchase the client never acknowledged inside the 3-day deadline, and it is already on the surviving SKUs: skip.ad.permanent (×3), skip.ad.30days (×3), plus auto.blessing (×2) which is not an ad-removal SKU at all. Removing the .sale packs cannot fix this.

7 · Verdict, the counter-argument, and what would falsify it

Keep it removed — conditional on fixing the acknowledgement path

Why not revert. There is no measurable revenue to win back: the net effect is +0.0277 $/install over D0–D2 (CI -0.1382 … +0.1993) and -0.0863 over D0–D7 (CI -0.2949 … +0.1322) — a range of roughly −$116 to $168/week at today's install rate, against $241/week of .sale revenue destroyed. Meanwhile reverting restores a SKU that refunds at 9.4% on player complaints of non-delivery, with the underlying defect still live.

Why "conditional" and not simply keep. The reason normally given for keeping it removed — fewer refunds — is false on this data: refunds rose (3.7% → 5.4%), and the volume migrated to a worse product at the same price. So the decision must be re-taken after the acknowledgement fix ships, using this same page. If the fix works and refunds on the ad-removal family fall below the catalogue rate, the choice becomes purely the revenue question — and at that point restoring one $10.99 permanent pack (not the three-SKU sale bundle) is the better product and the better price point.

What to do in the next build: (1) fix the acknowledgement/consume path for one-time products and verify against the three skip.ad.permanent and three skip.ad.30days orders above; (2) confirm the .sale grant path is genuinely dead rather than merely unsold; (3) re-run this page two weeks after the fix.

The strongest counter-argument to this verdict

The "revenue-neutral" conclusion depends on crediting a $0.07/install ad-revenue gain to the removal. It probably isn't the removal's. 60% of that gain is revenue per impression ($60.32 → $96.39), which no shop-card change can cause, and the two arms are dominated by different builds (v24/v25 vs v34) with known fill differences. Strip the ad line and the removal costs −$0.04/install over D0–D2 and −$0.13 over D0–D7 — about −$112 to −$35/week, roughly −$5,832 to −$1,808/year, in exchange for nothing except a worse substitute product. On that reading the right answer is to restore a single $10.99 permanent pack as soon as the acknowledgement bug is fixed. Note also that the discounted pack expanded the market the last time the price mix moved (v21: combined ad-removal buyers rose 9.3 → 10.5 per 1,000 users), so the removal may be suppressing demand rather than redirecting it.

What would falsify this verdict

  • Clean ad attribution. Ad revenue per DAU and fill rate by build in the same calendar week (v29 vs v34 active users). If v34's advantage disappears when the week is held fixed, the ad gain belongs to the removal and the neutral reading stands. If it holds, the removal cost −$73/week and reverting is right.
  • A holdout. Ship a build that keeps the removal but exposes skip.ad.permanent at $10.99 to a random half of new installs for two weeks. That measures the price elasticity and the substitution directly; no observational cut can, because the arms here are consecutive time periods with different traffic.
  • After the acknowledgement fix: if refunds on skip.ad.permanent and skip.ad.30days fall below the catalogue rate while permanent.sale's not_received/defective cluster persists, the defect is .sale-specific and the removal should stand on its own merit. If it does not persist, the refund rationale for the removal was never real.
  • Whale and mix checks already done: excluding the top payer moves the D0–D2 net from +0.0277 to +0.0212, and campaign-mix reweighting to +0.0018. Neither reopens the case for reverting.

8 · Traps respected (and what I could not establish)

  • Reused, not re-derived: the offer window, the 36.7% break-even framing, the v21 precedent, the refund-reason mix and the China spike were taken from the 2026-09-18/09-25/10-02 work and re-checked here rather than redone.
  • Never scored on active users. Every comparison is install cohort × matched days-since-install, because the offer only exists in a new player's first days and a version cut on active users is an update-speed selection artifact.
  • Gross revenue. event_value_in_usd is gross of refunds and VAT-inclusive outside the US; refunds come from Play's API, not GA4, and are reported separately. GA4 also misses billing-only app instances, so its absolute revenue level is slightly low — Play's order counts in section 4 are the authoritative totals.
  • Not established: (a) whether the post-arm ad gain is behavioural or a build effect — the decomposition points at build, but the two are not separable without a same-week build comparison; (b) whether the .sale refunds were a delivery bug or refund abuse — GA4 has no refund event and the client-side grant telemetry is a known partial instrumentation (9–73% per SKU), so this needs a client/Play check of one order's acknowledgement state; (c) why skip.ad.3days, skip.ad.10days and skip.ad.30days.sale also stopped selling in the week of 09-14, a week before v32 shipped — Play shows their last orders on 09-09/09-10/09-15, so there was a second shop change outside the v32 removal that this page does not explain; (d) whether the D0–D7 point estimate (-0.0863) or the D0–D2 one (+0.0277) is closer to the truth — the two windows use different (nested) install sets, and the honest answer is that the data cannot separate them.
