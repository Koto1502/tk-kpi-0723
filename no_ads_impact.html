Should we revert the no-ads sale-pack removal?

v32 (live 2026-09-21) removed skip.ad.permanent.sale ($10.99), skip.ad.30days.sale ($4.99) and skip.ad.annual.sale ($4.99). Data through 2026-10-10 — 19 post-removal days, 2,287 v32+ installs, up from 255 installs / 3 payers on 2026-09-25. The offer boundary is empirically v31 | v32: the last .sale purchase anywhere in the export is 2026-09-21 (Play's own order record), and every build from v32 on shows zero .sale taps, purchases or grant payouts.

In plain language

What's happening. In v32 (21 September) we removed the three discounted “no-ads” packs. The players who used to buy the $10.99 one did not move up to the $29.99 pack — we needed 37% of them to trade up for the change to pay for itself, and about 0% did. They moved sideways instead, to the surviving $10.99 30-day pack, which gives less for the same money and gets refunded at 32% of orders against 9% for the pack we removed. Net effect: roughly $241–$267 a week of discounted no-ads revenue disappeared, and nothing we can measure replaced it. The one number that made the removal look harmless — ad revenue went up — turns out to be a property of the new build, not of the shop change: v34 earns $78.46 per 1,000 ad impressions against $36.68 for v29 in the same weeks.

What should happen. Put the $10.99 permanent no-ads pack back as the new-player offer — the price point was working, and permanent is the better product than the 30-day pack that absorbed the volume. Separately, fix the bug that makes Google auto-refund purchases the client never acknowledged at exactly 72.17 hours after the charge; that bug is real, it is not specific to these packs, it is still live on the packs that remain, and restoring the offer neither causes nor cures it. Then re-measure.

This reverses the direction of the two earlier reads of this page. 2026-09-25 said no-ads revenue per install was down 70–75% but called the net “not yet significant”; the 2026-10-10 draft said keep it removed. Both leaned on an ad-revenue increase as the offsetting term. Section 4 tests that term directly and withdraws it — it is a property of the v32+ ad stack, not of the removal. With it withdrawn, every window's point estimate is negative, the destroyed revenue line is the only precisely measured term, and the trade-up thesis that justified the change fails by a factor of ten.

Verdict: roll it back — restore ONE $10.99 permanent no-ads pack

This recommendation rests on three things that do not depend on the design-sensitive net-per-install estimate, followed by the two disclosures that make the page defensible (section 2). Restore skip.ad.permanent.sale — permanent ad removal plus the same 20,000 gems as the $29.99 twin, at $10.99, as the standing new-player offer. Do not reinstate all three SKUs: skip.ad.annual.sale has never been tapped or bought once in the entire export, and skip.ad.30days.sale had already stopped selling of its own accord on 09-15. skip.ad.30days is a candidate to prune or fix on delivery grounds (it refunds at 31.6%).

  1. The trade-up definitively did not happen. Break-even needed ≥36.6% of .sale buyers to re-convert at $29.99; observed -3.6% (CI -18.9% … +12.4%) — the interval excludes break-even in every window. Play's order record agrees: the $29.99 twin went 14 → 8 orders, while the $10.99 30-day pack went 10 → 19. Buyers took 30 days instead of permanent at the same price. The demand was price-driven.
  2. The destroyed revenue has no demonstrated replacement. .sale revenue fell −$0.15 per install over D0–D2 (90% CI −$0.18 … −$0.11) — a 66% collapse of the no-ads IAP line — worth $241–$267/week ex-China depending on whether devices with no first_open are counted ($586–$648 over 17 days; August ran $280–632/wk). The offsetting terms are either not significant (other IAP $0.06, CI −$0.04 … $0.18) or withdrawn below as a build artifact (ads).
  3. What replaced it is a worse product at the same price. The same $10.99 now buys 30 days of no ads instead of permanent, and skip.ad.30days refunds at 31.6% against 9.4% for the pack that was removed (Fisher p = 0.026); order volume only partially transferred (19 post-removal orders in 20 days against 64 before).

Supporting, not decisive — the net-per-install estimate. With the ad term withdrawn the primary estimate is −$0.04/install over D0–D2 (CI −$0.20 … $0.12) and −$0.13 over D0–D7 (CI −$0.33 … $0.07) — −$112 to −$35/week at today's 843 installs/week, −$225 to −$70/week at the 1,691/week the game ran before the removal. Every one of those intervals spans zero, the design confounds the removal with calendar time, and a version-defined split of the same data says the opposite (section 2). Do not lead with this number; it is corroborating, not load-bearing.

Separate action item, not a condition of this verdict: the acknowledgement bug is real, unfixed, and hits the surviving full-price SKUs — 10 Google-initiated refunds, every one 72.167–72.173 h after the charge (skip.ad.permanent ×3, skip.ad.30days ×3, skip.ad.30days.sale ×1, skip.ad.permanent.sale ×1, auto.blessing ×2), which is Play auto-refunding a purchase the client never acknowledged. It is not .sale-specific, restoring the offer neither causes nor cures it, and it must be fixed regardless of this decision (section 7). The strongest argument against rolling back is in section 8.

-- Did the removal ship, and on which build? Every no-ads signal by app version,
-- 2026-09-14 .. 2026-10-12 (v32 went live 2026-09-21).
-- `earn` is the decisive column: `position` holds the SKU for shop grants, so it is the
-- client paying out the reward and cannot fire from a shop card that no longer exists.
WITH e AS (
  SELECT app_info.version v, event_name, user_pseudo_id u, PARSE_DATE('%Y%m%d', event_date) d,
    event_value_in_usd usd,
    COALESCE((SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='product_id'),
             (SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='position')) sku
  FROM `tiny-knightfall-idle-rpg-game.analytics_538412813.events_intraday_*`
  WHERE _TABLE_SUFFIX BETWEEN '20260914' AND '20261012'
)
SELECT v AS version, MIN(d) first_day, MAX(d) last_day,
  COUNT(DISTINCT u) users,
  COUNTIF(event_name='first_open') installs,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='purchase_click')  sale_taps,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='in_app_purchase') sale_buys,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='earn')            sale_grants,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='iap_purchase_success') sale_success,
  ROUND(SUM(IF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='in_app_purchase', usd, 0)), 2) sale_usd,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='purchase_click')   full_taps,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='in_app_purchase')  full_buys,
  ROUND(SUM(IF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='in_app_purchase', usd, 0)), 2) full_usd,
  COUNTIF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad.annual.sale%' AND event_name IN ('purchase_click','in_app_purchase','earn')) annual_sale_signals,
  MAX(IF(sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale' AND event_name='in_app_purchase', d, NULL)) last_sale_buy
FROM e
GROUP BY v
HAVING users >= 5
ORDER BY first_day

2 · Design — install cohorts at matched days since install, and the two things wrong with that

Claim. Because the offer only ever sold to players in their first few days, the fair comparison is install cohorts scored over the same days-since-install window, with China excluded and only completed windows counted. Active-user cuts are unusable for the effect — early auto-updaters are the engaged core, i.e. the payers — and a version-defined cut is unusable too, for the reason in the disclosures at the end of this section. Section 4 uses active users deliberately, for a different question (are the arms' ad economics a property of the build?), where who the users are cannot change revenue per impression.

Windows are D0, D0–D2 and D0–D7; each arm is restricted to installs whose whole window finished by 2026-10-09 (10-10 is an intraday, partial shard), so the D0–D7 arm is smaller than the D0–D2 arm by construction — which is why the two must never be compared to each other, only to their own offer-present baseline. Two traps are handled explicitly:

  • China is excluded from both arms. The 09-19 mirror/bot spike is ~300 installs/day on v29 with $0 IAP and $0 ads; left in, it would make the post arm look better and v29 look terrible. It is still 9.7% of post-removal installs (255), so the all-builds row above exists to show it makes no difference.
  • The 2026-09-28 09:00 → 09-29 03:00 UTC event gap (~18h, no events at all) sits inside the D0–D7 window of installs from 09-21 to 09-27, i.e. only the post arm. The gap-free row removes exactly those installs; the gap can only understate post revenue, which is the conservative direction for this verdict.
The per-user-day extract (5.0 GiB scan, cached):
-- Per user-day facts, 2026-08-01 .. 2026-10-12 (one row per device per active day).
-- Source: GA4 raw export, events_intraday_* shards only. Extended from
-- notebooks/sql/15_user_day_facts.sql with the no-ads SKU classes split out, so the
-- substitution question (did .sale buyers move to the full-price twin?) can be answered
-- on the same per-install-day grid without a second scan.
WITH ev AS (
  SELECT user_pseudo_id u, PARSE_DATE('%Y%m%d', event_date) d, event_name e,
    event_timestamp ts, geo.country country, app_info.version v, event_value_in_usd usd,
    -- `earn` carries the SKU in `position`, purchases in `product_id`; take both, in this order
    COALESCE((SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='product_id'),
             (SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='position')) sku,
    -- Impression-level ad revenue reported by AppLovin MAX.
    (SELECT COALESCE(value.double_value, value.float_value, CAST(value.int_value AS FLOAT64))
       FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='value') val,
    (SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(user_properties) WHERE key='appsflyer_id') af
  FROM `tiny-knightfall-idle-rpg-game.analytics_538412813.events_intraday_*`
  WHERE _TABLE_SUFFIX BETWEEN '20260801' AND '20261012'
)
SELECT u, d,
  ANY_VALUE(country) country,
  ARRAY_AGG(v IGNORE NULLS ORDER BY ts DESC LIMIT 1)[SAFE_OFFSET(0)] v,
  MAX(af) af,
  COUNTIF(e='first_open') first_opens,
  -- all IAP
  SUM(IF(e='in_app_purchase', usd, 0)) iap_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='in_app_purchase') iap_buys,
  -- ad-removal SKUs, all of them
  SUM(IF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%', usd, 0)) noads_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%') noads_buys,
  -- the discounted .sale variants removed in v32
  SUM(IF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale', usd, 0)) sale_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale') sale_buys,
  -- the full-price twin of the pack that mattered: skip.ad.permanent at $29.99
  SUM(IF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad.permanent%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale', usd, 0)) perm_full_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad.permanent%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale') perm_full_buys,
  -- the single pack that carried the volume: skip.ad.permanent.sale at $10.99
  SUM(IF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad.permanent.sale%', usd, 0)) perm_sale_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='in_app_purchase' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad.permanent.sale%') perm_sale_buys,
  -- card taps: purchase_click, the player opening the offer
  COUNTIF(e='purchase_click' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%') noads_taps,
  COUNTIF(e='purchase_click' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad.permanent%' AND sku NOT LIKE '%.sale') perm_full_taps,
  COUNTIF(e='purchase_click' AND sku LIKE '%skip.ad%' AND sku LIKE '%.sale') sale_taps,
  COUNTIF(e='purchase_click') shop_taps,
  -- ad revenue (impression-level) and impressions
  SUM(IF(e='ad_impression_MAX', COALESCE(val, 0), 0)) ad_usd,
  COUNTIF(e='ad_impression_MAX') ad_imps
FROM ev
GROUP BY u, d

Two design problems, stated up front — read these before any number below

1. This is not a natural experiment. The offer-present arm is installs 2026-09-04…09-20 and the offer-absent arm is 2026-09-21…10-07, so any calendar trend — UA campaigns ending, traffic quality, the 09-28 data gap, a store algorithm change — loads onto the treatment. Install cohorts at matched days-since-install remove the update-speed selection that ruins a version cut, but they cannot remove time. Every net-per-install interval on this page spans zero, and the arms' install rate differs by 2×.

2. A version-defined split cannot measure this effect, and its face value is misleading. It looks like the clean cut — “installs on a build that still has the offer” vs “installs on a build that does not” — but once v32 shipped, Play would not serve an older build to a new device, so a post-removal install on v31-or-older is almost never a real Play install. In this data 47.1% of that arm's installs are the known China mirror/bot APK (294 of 624), against 1.1% for v32+. The naive version split therefore reads IAP $0.06 per install for the arm whose client still has the offer versus $0.35 for the arm that lost it — which would argue for keeping the removal — while measuring a third-party APK spike, not the offer. It is disclosed here and used nowhere else in this document.

What that leaves

The per-install comparison below is the best available read, but it is corroboration, not the load-bearing argument. The argument is the failure of the trade-up plus the un-replaced revenue plus the worse replacement product.

4 · The decisive test — is the ad-revenue gain the removal, or the build?

Claim: the build. The offer-absent arm earns more ad revenue per install, and the earlier reads of this page treated that as the removal's doing — players who cannot buy ad removal watch more ads. The same-week-by-build query:
-- Same-calendar-week ad economics by build — the decisive attribution test.
--
-- The question: the offer-absent arm earns more ad revenue per install than the
-- offer-present arm. Is that the removal (players who cannot buy ad-removal watch more
-- ads) or a property of the build (v32+ has much better rewarded fill than v29)?
--
-- Holding the calendar week fixed and grouping by build answers it, because a shop-card
-- change cannot alter revenue *per impression*. The window deliberately starts before the
-- removal so the same build gradient can be measured while the offer was still on sale.
WITH ev AS (
  SELECT app_info.version v, user_pseudo_id u, PARSE_DATE('%Y%m%d', event_date) d,
    event_name e, geo.country country,
    (SELECT COALESCE(value.double_value, value.float_value, CAST(value.int_value AS FLOAT64))
       FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='value') val,
    (SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='ad_type') ad_type,
    (SELECT value.string_value FROM UNNEST(event_params) WHERE key='status') status
  FROM `tiny-knightfall-idle-rpg-game.analytics_538412813.events_intraday_*`
  WHERE _TABLE_SUFFIX BETWEEN '20260901' AND '20261012'
    AND event_name IN ('ad_impression_MAX', 'ad_request_status', 'ad_reward')
)
SELECT DATE_TRUNC(d, WEEK(MONDAY)) wk, v AS version, country,
  COUNT(DISTINCT CONCAT(u, '|', CAST(d AS STRING))) user_days,
  COUNTIF(e = 'ad_impression_MAX') imps,
  ROUND(SUM(IF(e = 'ad_impression_MAX', COALESCE(val, 0), 0)), 2) ad_usd,
  COUNTIF(e = 'ad_request_status' AND ad_type = 'rewarded') rew_req,
  COUNTIF(e = 'ad_request_status' AND ad_type = 'rewarded' AND status = 'success') rew_ok,
  COUNTIF(e = 'ad_reward') rewards
FROM ev
GROUP BY wk, version, country
ORDER BY wk, version, country

7 · Refunds — a separate action item, not a condition of the rollback

Claim. Two distinct delivery defects are visible in Play's own records. Neither is caused by the .sale SKUs and neither was fixed by removing them — the volume simply migrated onto a pack that refunds worse. Restoring the discounted offer is therefore not a trade against this problem, and it is not a substitute for fixing it: put the rollback and the purchase-completion fix in the same release.

What the removal did to refunds: it removed one SKU that refunded at 9.4% and replaced its volume with a SKU that refunds at 31.6% (Fisher p = 0.026). The game's refund rate went up, 3.7% → 5.4%, and the increase is entirely inside the ad-removal family (non-ad-removal: 2.3% → 2.9%).

Defect 1 — the client never acknowledges one-time-product purchases. 10 refunds in the ledger were initiated by Google, and every single one lands between 72.167 h and 72.173 h after the charge (mean 72.169 h) — a machine, not a player. That is the signature of Play's automatic refund for a purchase the client never acknowledged inside the 3-day deadline. By SKU: skip.ad.permanent ×3, skip.ad.30days ×3, skip.ad.30days.sale ×1, skip.ad.permanent.sale ×1 — i.e. one of the ten is on the pack that was removed and six are on the two SKUs that remain on sale — plus auto.blessing ×2, which is not an ad-removal SKU at all. Removing the .sale packs cannot fix this, and it is not .sale-specific.

8 · Verdict, the counter-argument, and what would falsify it

Roll back: restore ONE $10.99 permanent ad-removal pack (skip.ad.permanent.sale)

Why roll back. Three things point the same way, and none of them depends on the one term that made the earlier reads look neutral:

  • The destroyed revenue is real and precisely measured. $0.15/install over D0–D2 (CI −$0.18 … −$0.11), $241/week ex-China — a 66% collapse of the no-ads IAP line, and 36.7% of tracked IAP revenue historically.
  • The change did not do what it was for. The trade-up failed by a factor of ten (-3.6% against a 36.6% break-even) and the refund problem it was credited with fixing got worse (3.7% → 5.4%, with the non-ad-removal catalogue flat). Nothing the removal was supposed to buy actually happened.
  • The compensation was bookkeeping, not behaviour. The ad gain is a property of the v32+ ad stack (section 4). Once withdrawn, the point estimate is negative in every window: −$0.04/install over D0–D2, −$0.13 over D0–D7 — −$112 to −$35/week at today's 843 installs/week, −$225 to −$70/week at the 1,691/week the game was running before the removal.

Why this form and not the three-SKU bundle. The price point was never the problem — the twin at $29.99 converts at roughly a tenth of the rate (8 Play orders in 20 post-removal days against 64 for the $10.99 pack in the 20 before it). What the surviving $10.99 offer got wrong is the product: 30 days instead of permanent, refunding at 31.6% (Fisher p = 0.026). So restore the permanent form at $10.99 inside the same new-player window. Leave skip.ad.annual.sale deleted — never tapped or bought once in the entire export — and leave skip.ad.30days.sale deleted unless the 30-day form is wanted for a different job.

How much it should recover: the mirror of the measured loss — $0.04 to $0.13 per install, i.e. roughly −$112 to −$35/week at today's volumes, plus the product-quality gain from selling permanence rather than a 30-day rental. Treat that as an expected value, not a promise: install volume is now half what it was and the traffic mix has changed.

What rolling back does NOT do. It does not fix the acknowledgement bug, and it is not a substitute for fixing it: 10 Google-initiated refunds land exactly 72.17 h after the charge on the surviving full-price SKUs (section 7). Restoring the pack adds a SKU that refunds at 9.4% on player complaints of non-delivery, so the two actions belong in the same release: restore the offer and fix the purchase-completion path, then confirm both against this page.

Honesty about the strength of this call: every net point estimate here has a 90% interval that spans zero. The reason to act anyway is that the individual pieces are not symmetric — the destroyed line is measured with a tight interval, the mechanism behind the offsetting ad term is disproved, and the stated rationale for the removal is falsified. That is a preponderance-of-evidence recommendation, not a statistically significant effect.

The strongest argument against rolling back

Two things, and they are why this is written as a preponderance call. First, the raw D0–D2 point estimate is positive — total revenue per install +0.0277 (CI −$0.14 … $0.20), and campaign-mix reweighted +0.0018. If even part of the ad gain is real behaviour rather than the build — players who cannot buy ad removal watching more rewarded ads, which the data cannot rule out because build and removal are collinear on the install side — then the removal is closer to neutral than to a loss, and a rollback would be undoing a change that paid for itself. Second, the arms are consecutive time periods, not a randomisation: UA campaigns ended, traffic quality moved (post-removal D1 retention was ~5 pts lower), and the install rate halved, so no observational cut here is a clean counterfactual. If the removal's real purpose was to stop refund abuse rather than to reprice, the case for undoing it must rest on the refund numbers, and those are small (13 post-removal refunds against 17 before).

What would falsify this verdict

  • A holdout, the only clean test. Ship a build that exposes skip.ad.permanent at $10.99 to a random half of new installs for two weeks. If the treated half does not out-earn the control, the destroyed .sale line was being replaced by something the measurement cannot see, and the rollback is wrong.
  • Ad attribution, measured properly. Ad revenue per DAU and rewarded fill by build within the same calendar week, with enough old-build users to control for country. Section 4 already shows the per-impression gap is a build property ($78.46 vs $36.68 per 1,000 impressions at 79.1% vs 62.7% fill); if a properly powered country-controlled version showed the gap vanishes, the removal's neutrality would be back on the table.
  • Price elasticity, measured directly. The v21 precedent cuts against the removal (combined ad-removal buyers rose 9.3 → 10.5 per 1,000 users when the mix flipped to $10.99). If a test of $10.99 against $29.99 showed the $29.99 tier converting comparably once the offer is present, restoring the discount would be unnecessary.
  • Refund causality. If a client audit shows permanent.sale's not_received/defective refunds were refund abuse rather than a delivery fault, the case for keeping that specific SKU out of the catalogue strengthens; if they were a grant bug, the fix is client-side and restoring the pack carries no extra refund risk.
  • Already checked, and neither reopens the case for keeping it removed: excluding the top payer moves the D0–D2 primary estimate from −$0.04 to −$0.05, and campaign-mix reweighting to −$0.03.

9 · Traps respected (and what I could not establish)

  • Reused, not re-derived: the offer window, the 36.7% break-even framing, the v21 precedent, the refund-reason mix and the China spike were taken from the 2026-09-18/09-25/10-02 work and re-checked here rather than redone.
  • Never scored on active users for the effect. Every effect comparison is install cohort × matched days-since-install, because the offer only exists in a new player's first days and a version cut on active users is an update-speed selection artifact. The one active-user comparison (section 4) asks a build-property question — revenue per impression, which who the users are cannot change — and it is labelled as such. The version-defined split is disclosed in section 2 and used nowhere.
  • Gross revenue. event_value_in_usd is gross of refunds and VAT-inclusive outside the US; refunds come from Play's API, not GA4, and are reported separately. GA4 also misses billing-only app instances, so its absolute revenue level is slightly low — Play's order counts in section 5 are the authoritative totals.

The same-week build test settles the per-impression half of the ad question (section 4), but a residual behavioural ad component cannot be isolated because build and removal are perfectly collinear on the install side, and the install-cohort design confounds the removal with calendar time.
