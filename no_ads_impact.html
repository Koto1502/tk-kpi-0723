14 · Impact of removing the no-ads sale packs¶

Question. The .sale no-ads packs (skip.ad.permanent.sale $10.99, skip.ad.30days.sale, skip.ad.annual.sale) were removed from the client. When did that actually ship, and what did it do to revenue from new players?

Project tiny-knightfall-idle-rpg-game.analytics_538412813 · run 2026-09-25 · follows 12_no_ads_sale_pack_removal.ipynb (2026-09-18), which found the removal had not shipped in v28/v29.

Design. The .sale packs are a new-player offer — 98% of their purchases land within 2 days of install, none after day 6 — so removing them changes the new-player funnel. The fair comparison is install cohorts at matched days since install, not a version cut of active users (dominated by update-speed selection).

  • offer present = installs 2026-09-10 → 09-20 (every build then live — 26.2, 28, 29 — still sold the packs; section 1)
  • offer absent = installs whose first_open build is v32 / v33 / v34
  • China excluded from both. A China install spike starts 09-19 (≈300/day vs ≈5 before), lands almost entirely on v29 and barely monetises. It is 45% of v29 installs but <1% of v32+, so leaving it in makes the removal look like it doubled revenue. See section 2.
  • windows D0, D0–D1, D0–D2, each only over installs whose last window day is complete (≤ 2026-09-24; 09-25 is intraday) — so the short windows use more of the post-removal data.

Caveats. Revenue = event_value_in_usd on in_app_purchase (gross; non-US VAT included; no refunds in the export). Ad revenue = value on ad_impression_MAX. Not randomised: the arms are consecutive weeks with a different campaign mix, so section 4 reweights by campaign. The post-removal arm has a few hundred installs and a handful of payers — read the intervals.

6 · Conclusion¶

The removal shipped in v32 (live 2026-09-21). v32, v33 and v34 show zero .sale taps, purchases, reward grants or purchase successes across ~950 users and ~550 installs, while v28/v29 were still selling them through today. Nothing is gone from the older builds — players who stay on v29 still see the offer.

Early read (ex-China, new installs, D0–D1 — the window with the most post-removal data):

per new install, D0–D1 offer present offer absent (v32+) change mix-adjusted
no-ads IAP $ 0.202 0.054 −0.148 (90% CI −0.25 … −0.03) −0.132
other IAP $ 0.143 0.215 +0.072 (−0.12 … +0.30) +0.034
ad revenue $ 0.073 0.077 +0.004 −0.007
total $ 0.418 0.345 −0.072 (−0.35 … +0.24) −0.104
  • The one clear effect: early no-ads revenue fell ~70–75%. .sale buyers were 10.2 per 1,000 installs by D1; the full-price no-ads SKUs picked up only 3.9 per 1,000 after (2.5 before).
  • Players are looking for the offer. No-ads taps per install rose ~2.4× (26.5 → 62.7 per 1k) — they open the no-ads card, see $29.99 instead of $10.99, and mostly don't buy.
  • Some substitution on install day: full-price no-ads buyers went 2.0 → 7.2 per 1k on D0 — but that is 3 buyers, so it cannot yet tell 15% substitution from 80%.
  • Total revenue per install: down ~$0.07–0.10 (≈ 17–29%), but not statistically distinguishable from zero.** Other IAP and ad revenue have not visibly compensated. At ~1,100 non-China installs a week that is roughly **−$80–115/week of first-2-day revenue, concentrated in the no-ads line.
  • Ads per install did not rise — so far no sign that players who can no longer cheaply remove ads are generating more ad revenue in their first days (it would show later, if at all).

How much to trust it. The post-removal arm is 255 installs with 3 payers over D0–D1 (417 / 6 on D0; 102 / 3 on D0–D2). The no-ads drop is robust; the net-revenue direction is not. The arms are consecutive weeks and the later one has more Tier 1 ROAS traffic (reweighting makes the drop slightly larger). Gross revenue only; no refunds in the export — and permanent.sale was the most-refunded SKU (7 of 20 Play refunds in 30 days), so some of the lost gross was never kept.

China install spike — exclude it everywhere. From 2026-09-19 China jumped from ~5 to ~300 installs/day, 98% on v29, with $0.00 IAP and $0.00 ad revenue per install over D0–D2. It looks like non-Play (mirror/bot) traffic, and it is inflating install counts on the dashboards.
