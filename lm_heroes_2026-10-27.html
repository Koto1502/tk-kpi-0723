Lucky Machine, hero packs and heroes per player

Tiny Knightfall · Android, non-China · GA4 export through 2026-10-26 · built 2026-10-27

Answers

  1. Lucky Machine was not a measurable net improvement in ARPU, ARPPU or retention. For stage ≥ 31 players, ARPDAU went $0.28 → $0.33 → $0.29 (before / during / after). The during-vs-before gap is $0.05 (90% CI −$0.13 … $0.24), and it is two or three large buyers: payers were 27 / 27 / 26. Veterans came back at the normal rate, and players who reached stage 31 during the event retained no better (D7 16.5%, vs 23.8% before and 18.6% after; the lower 'before' gap follows the late-September traffic change). Its own revenue was $91.72 from 8 pack purchases (6.3% of IAP in its window); the two ticket packs sold 0.
  2. Bring it back, but as a measured test, not as a proven earner, and don't build 2–3 more early events yet. The stage-31 gate did reach players: 40–49% of stage 31–100 players spun. They did not pay, though: every pack dollar came from stage ≥ 101, and early reachers' retention didn't move. An early event is cheap engagement, not revenue. Re-run Lucky Machine with a remote-config holdout and the two defects fixed, and judge the early-event idea on that result.
  3. New hero packs: the Nun pack works, the rest are small, and none of them grew the payer base. The Nun pack ($9.99) sold to 23 buyers / $248 in 5 weeks, the best new pack. The other four together made $136. 1 of 31 buyers made their first-ever purchase on a hero pack; buyers are deep (median stage ~330) existing payers, and ARPDAU and payers per 1,000 DAU did not rise after launch. So they mostly re-route existing payers' money.
  4. Heroes per player: almost everyone has only the starter. Estimated, because no event logs a hero. 68.7% of players who reach stage 31 own only Pom (mean 1.45 of 16); payers average 2.3, mostly because a first purchase grants Knight. The free routes barely land: Volt (5 ads) reaches 18.7% of stage-31 players and Sai (7-day login) 2.6%. Each pack hero is owned by under 2%.

Why the event couldn't show much. (1) Reach: on day 1 only 11.2% of players were on an event build (v28/29/32/33/34), rising to 80.4% by day 10. (2) Two defects reported on 10-02: ad tickets granted without an ad (87% of ad-ticket grants have no ad), and a check-in double claim (41 players). Free tickets compete with the ticket packs. (3) Spin prizes and pack grants are not logged, so the economy can't be audited. (4) There was no holdout. Every comparison here is before/after, so a +20% effect would be invisible at this scale (~27 payers per 10 days).

Re-run recommendation

  • Re-run it: it is already built and costs little. Ship it on one build that is ≥ 90% of DAU before the start date.
  • Fix the ad-ticket grant (ticket only after ad_impression_MAX on rewareded_mini_event) and the check-in double claim.
  • Hold out 20% of eligible players by remote config (event hidden). That turns the next run into an answer: ARPDAU, payer rate and D7 for event vs holdout.
  • Log spin prizes and pack grants (earn with the SKU as position, as every other pack does).
  • Reconsider giving away Leo at 10,000★ while the Divine Cat pack sells him at $19.99, or make the milestone reachable only with paid tickets.

2. Should we add 2–3 more events triggered this early?

Not yet. The stage-31 gate brought in players (40–49% of stage 31–100 players spun vs ~30% at 101+), but none of them paid for the event and their retention didn't improve. Two or three more events of the same kind would add engagement we can't show is worth anything. What would change this: the Lucky Machine re-run with a holdout showing a D7 or payer-rate gain for stage 31–100 players.
  • Gate reach (unlock report, v34 installs, by day 3): stage 30 = 14% of installs, stage 65 = 5.5%, stage 101 ≈ 3%. An early gate reaches about 4× the players, but they are the ones who don't pay yet.
  • Where the money is: every Lucky Machine pack dollar came from stage ≥ 101 players. The 10-14 sizing found stage ≥ 101 players are ~25% of DAU and 61% of IAP.
  • Dice Adventure and Fishing (planned, gate 101) already target the paying group. Run them as planned, each with the same holdout and grant logging, rather than adding new early events.
  • If the goal is early retention, the problem is elsewhere: the v36/v37 difficulty wall at stage 34 (losses 10% → 40–45%) and the D1 cliff around stage 24. An event at stage 31 sits right in front of that wall.

3. How effective are the new hero packs?

The Nun pack is the one that works (23 buyers, $248, ~$51/week). Hero's Growth, SS Hero + Toys, Soulstone and the Divine Cat pack together made $136. They have not grown revenue overall: buyers are existing, deep payers, and game-wide ARPDAU and payers per 1,000 DAU are flat across the launch weeks.

Did buyers spend more overall, or just on the hero pack?

23 buyers whose first new-hero purchase was on or before 10-12: other IAP in the 14 days before = $38.74 per buyer, 14 days after = $18.11, plus $13.70 on the hero packs themselves, so $31.81 in total after vs $38.74 before. Their total spend did not go up. The hero pack took part of what they were already spending, which matches the flat game-wide numbers.

Why. Hero packs sit behind deep gates (Nun: mine depth 40; Soulstone: stage 115; Hero + Toys: stage 251), so only ~5–10% of DAU can see them, and those players already pay. A hero pack can only grow the payer base if a new or mid-game player can see it. The Divine Cat pack ($19.99, Leo) is ungated but has sold 2. Its price is the likely problem (Selective Hero at $24.99 sold 14, mostly on day 0–3).

5. What we suggest

  1. Re-run Lucky Machine as an experiment: one ≥ 90% build, defects fixed, 20% remote-config holdout, prizes and grants logged. Decide on more early events from its result.
  2. Keep the event calendar on the paying group: Dice Adventure, then Fishing (gate 101), each with the same holdout. That is where the measurable money is.
  3. Fix the early wall before adding early content. Stage 34 (v36/v37) and the stage-24 D1 cliff decide early retention more than any event can.
  4. Hero monetisation: bring a hero offer forward. The Nun at $9.99 proves the appetite; it is just gated deep. Test a mid-game SS hero offer (stage 31–65, $4.99–9.99, 48 h) and watch first-purchase rate. That is the one lever here that could grow payers, not just ARPPU.
  5. Price-test Divine Cat (Leo) at $9.99 (it sold 2 at $19.99), and stop handing Leo out free at 10,000★ in the same period.
  6. Give every player a second hero early. 68.7% of stage-31 players still have only Pom; Volt needs 5 opt-in ads, and only ~9% of DAU watch any ad. Granting Volt (or a pick) from a main quest around stage 10–20 opens the roster loop that Hero's Growth, fragments and hero packs sell into.
  7. Log hero acquisition (earn HERO_<id>), so the next report counts heroes instead of estimating them.

Method and data

  • Source: GA4 export, non-China, 2026-07-20 … 10-26. SQL dashboard/lm_hero_sql/user_days.sql (3.7 GiB) and store.sql. Builder dashboard/build_lm_hero_report.py.
  • Stage cleared = level_win(N) − 1. Lucky Machine gate = campaign stage 31, builds 28/29/32/33/34, 09-16 … 09-25. Rewards and gates from the v37 client data (2-EventSlotMachineData, HeroData, SevenDaysLoginData, pack assets).
  • Revenue = Play-billed in_app_purchase gross (incl. VAT) + MAX ad value. Export gap days (09-28, 10-18 … 10-20) excluded from per-day rates.
  • Build-based control groups were tried and rejected. Players who stayed on old builds during the event were already leaving (2 active days of 10, 25% returned), so comparing them with updaters measures who updates, not the event.
